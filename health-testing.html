Breeding Ethics & Rigor

Health & DNA Testing

Producing healthy, long-lived service dogs begins at the genetic level. Discover how Summer’s Dogs utilizes comprehensive Embark DNA, PennHIP, and OFA screenings.

Genetics

DNA Testing with Embark

Embark is one of the premier canine DNA panels available, testing for over 230+ genetic health risks. Genetic testing is the gold standard for responsible breeding to ensure puppies inherit the healthiest possible genetics.

How Gene Copies Work:

• 2 Copies (Affected): The dog has the genetic health condition and will pass a copy to 100% of their offspring. Dogs with 2 copies of severe hereditary disorders are never bred.

• 1 Copy (Carrier): The dog is healthy and unaffected, but carries a 50% chance of passing the gene copy. Carriers may be safely bred, but strictly to clear (0 copy) mates so no puppy can ever be affected.

• 0 Copies (Clear): The dog is entirely free of the variant and cannot pass it on. Clear dogs are the foundation of healthy breeding.

230+ Health Variants Screened

• Degenerative Myelopathy (DM)

• Multi-Drug Resistance (MDR1)

• Canine Hyperuricosuria (HUU)

• Von Willebrand Disease (vWD)

Joint Health

PennHIP Joint Laxity Testing

Unlike the subjective single-view OFA radiograph, PennHIP requires deep sedation/anesthesia and captures three precise radiographs to calculate the distraction index (DI)—a scientifically validated measurement of passive hip joint laxity.

Scores range between 0 and 1, with 0 indicating exceptionally tight, healthy hips and 1 indicating severe laxity. Veterinary research demonstrates that distraction indices provide a vastly superior predictor of osteoarthritis vulnerability, allowing Summer’s Dogs to select only the soundest breeding lines.

Orthopedic Foundation for Animals

OFA Hips & Elbows Certification

Rigorous radiographic evaluation verified by an independent panel of board-certified veterinary radiologists.

OFA Hip Classification

The Orthopedic Foundation classifies hip joint conformation into seven distinct categories:

  • Excellent: Superior joint conformation, deep congruent acetabulum.
  • Good: Well-formed joint with full femoral head coverage.
  • Fair: Minor irregularities but fully within normal limits.
  • Borderline: Inconclusive findings requiring re-evaluation.
  • Mild, Moderate & Severe: Dysplastic joints excluded from breeding.

OFA Elbow Dysplasia Grading

Elbow dysplasia evaluates fragmented medial coronoid (FCP), osteochondritis (OCD), and ununited anconeal processes (UAP):

  • Normal: Entirely clean, healthy joint without osteophyte formation.
  • Grade I: Minimal bone change (<2mm) along anconeal process.
  • Grade II: Moderate proliferation (2–5mm) and subchondral sclerosis.
  • Grade III: Well-developed joint disease (>5mm bone proliferation).
