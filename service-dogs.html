Disability Mitigation

Service Dog Training

Task Trained Dogs to mitigate their handler’s disability. Helping individuals with Autism, PTSD, and other disabilities achieve greater independence.

Self-Evaluation

Is a Service Dog Right for You?

This can be a hard question. Not only do you have to have a qualifying disability that would be mitigated by a Service Dog under the ADA, but you also have to have the correct lifestyle and resources.

1

Why do you want & need a Service Dog?

If the answer is you just want a companion or pet buddy, a Service Dog might not be the right fit for you. A service dog is medical equipment trained to perform specific life-mitigating tasks.

2

Would a Service Dog fit with your family?

If you have other pets that are untrained, reactive, or aggressive, or if your living situation prevents regular exercise and maintenance, a service dog will struggle to succeed.

3

Do you have the lifetime funds?

While Summer’s Dogs sliding scale makes receiving and training a dog accessible, you must afford food, vet care, grooming, and equipment for the dog's entire 10-14 year life.

Requirements

The Training Commitment

Training Your Own Dog: When in the training program with your own dog, you will need to meet a minimum of once every 2 weeks for 2 hours of hands-on coaching for the length of the program. Average time of training is 1 year with a minimum of 6 months.

Receiving a Program Dog: When receiving a fully trained Service Dog from our breeding program, you will meet for a 3-hour interview and training session with your prospect at 1 year old, followed by monthly bonding and handling sessions until placement.

All handlers sign a comprehensive mutual agreement protecting both the handler and the organization.

Hourly Sessions

1 to 2 hours, 1–2x per week

Ideal for puppies, base manners, handler communication, and beginning obedience stages.

Daily Immersion

4 to 6 hours, 1–2x per week

Tailored for intensive public access training, high distraction environments, and task rehearsals.

Board & Train

Overnight Stays

Dedicated intensive task conditioning, behavior shaping, and task polishing with the master trainer.

Task Specialties

Autism & PTSD Specialized Tasks

Here at Summer’s Dogs we specialize in Autism and PTSD mitigation tasks:

🐾

Deep Pressure Therapy

Applying calm, soothing body pressure to regulate sensory overstimulation and panic attacks.

⚓

Grounding

Tactile focus, nudges, and physical positioning to bring handlers out of dissociation or flashbacks.

🔔

Cortisol Alert

Detecting biochemical cortisol and adrenaline spikes before full panic or meltdowns occur.

🛡️

Action Interruption

Interrupting repetitive behaviors, skin picking, scratching, or escalating anxiety loops.
